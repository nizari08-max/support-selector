Assumptions & Formulae Calculation Basis Span assumptions, limits, unit conversions, and governing equations.

Assumptions

  • All spans assume pipes are full of water and insulated. Insulation density = 200 kg/m³ (12.5 lb/ft³), insulation thickness = 50 mm (2 in).
  • Low Temperature Carbon Steel (LT) pipe weights and spans are taken as identical to Carbon Steel (CS).
  • Spans for CS and SS are governed by the lesser of: ½ in (12.7 mm) self-weight deflection limit, or 5000 psi (34.5 MPa) self-weight bending stress limit (whichever gives the smaller span).
  • All spans are for continuous horizontal straight runs only.
  • Seismic loads are NOT included. For seismic zones, increase the pipe weight by the seismic g-factor and reduce spans using the formulae below. Refer to the project Seismic Analysis of Piping Procedures.
  • Changes in direction, blast loading, elevated temperature beyond 400 °C, or passive fire protection will require shorter spans than those tabulated. Consult the stress engineer.
  • For fluids denser than water (e.g. slurry ≈ 2500 kg/m³ / 156 lb/ft³), the maximum allowable span must be recalculated by the stress engineer.
  • FRP spans are based on Fiberbond 20FR16 specifications with an allowable stress of 1000 psi. Maximum spans are hard-capped at 6.1 m (20 ft).
  • For individual applications such as flare lines, maximum allowable spans may be increased only with written approval from the piping stress engineer.

Governing Formulae

From Pipe Stress Engineering, L.C. Peng — Chapter: Strength of Materials Basics. The span is taken as the lesser value from both equations.

Deflection Limit

Δ = (3 · w · L⁴) / (384 · E · I)
Allowable: Δ ≤ 12.7 mm (½ in)

Bending Stress Limit

S = M / Z = (w · L²) / (10 · Z)
Allowable: S ≤ 34.5 MPa (5000 psi)
